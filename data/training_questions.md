# Selected group theory training questions

Selected by the user for future training. These are problem statements and learning
objectives; selection does not imply that Lean formalization or training is complete.
Original exercise numbers are preserved; the source textbook was not specified.

Throughout, $G$ is a group with identity $1$, and $|x|$ denotes the order of $x$.

## 1. Exercise 11 — Computing element orders · Easy

Find the order of every element of the additive group $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$.

**Value:** Builds concrete intuition for element order and cyclic groups.

## 2. Exercise 16 — Recognizing elements of order two · Easy

Let $x\in G$. Prove that $x^2=1$ if and only if $|x|$ is either $1$ or $2$.

**Value:** Practices translating between an algebraic equation and the definition of order.

## 3. Exercise 15 — Inverting a product · Easy–moderate

Prove that, for all $a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n\in G$,

$$
(a_1a_2\cdots a_n)^{-1}
=
a_n^{-1}a_{n-1}^{-1}\cdots a_1^{-1}.
$$

**Value:** Develops induction and careful manipulation of products in noncommutative groups.

## 4. Exercise 26 — The subgroup criterion · Moderate

Suppose $H$ is a nonempty subset of $G$ that is closed under multiplication and
inverses: for every $h,k\in H$, both $hk\in H$ and $h^{-1}\in H$.

Prove that $H$ is a group under the operation inherited from $G$. Such a subset is
called a subgroup of $G$.

**Value:** Establishes a reusable method for proving that a subset forms a group.

## 5. Exercise 27 — Constructing a cyclic subgroup · Moderate

Let $x\in G$. Prove that

$$
\langle x\rangle=\{x^n\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
$$

is a subgroup of $G$, called the cyclic subgroup generated by $x$.

**Value:** Applies the subgroup criterion and connects integer powers to group structure.

## 6. Exercise 25 — A condition forcing commutativity · Moderate

Prove that if $x^2=1$ for every $x\in G$, then $G$ is abelian.

**Value:** Trains you to extract a global structural conclusion from an identity
satisfied by every element.

## 7. Exercise 9 — Groups built from $\sqrt{2}$ · Moderate

Let

$$
G=\{a+b\sqrt{2}\in\mathbb{R}\mid a,b\in\mathbb{Q}\}.
$$

(a) Prove that $G$ is a group under addition.

(b) Prove that $G\setminus\{0\}$ is a group under multiplication.

**Value:** Practices closure and explicit inverse construction, preparing for field extensions.

## 8. Exercise 23 — Orders of powers · Moderate–hard

Suppose $x\in G$ has finite order $n$. If $n=st$ for positive integers $s,t$, prove that

$$
|x^s|=t.
$$

**Value:** Develops the distinction between finding an exponent that gives the
identity and proving that exponent is minimal.

## 9. Exercise 22 — Conjugation preserves order · Moderate–hard

For $x,g\in G$, prove that

$$
|x|=|g^{-1}xg|.
$$

Deduce that

$$
|ab|=|ba|
\qquad\text{for all }a,b\in G.
$$

**Value:** Introduces conjugation as a structure-preserving operation. The statement
includes elements of infinite order.

## 10. Exercise 31 — An element of order two must exist · Hard

Prove that every finite group $G$ of even order contains an element of order $2$.

**Value:** Combines the inverse operation with a counting argument—a useful first
example of proving existence without explicitly constructing an element.
